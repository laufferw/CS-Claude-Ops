# Prove the Value, Then Sell It

Ask a CS leader what stops their team from helping customers reach measurable outcomes, and the honest answers are uncomfortable. We can't track the usage that matters. Outcomes aren't really our job. We don't know which outcomes our customers want.

Each answer points at the same flaw. Customer Success exists to make customers successful in ways both sides can see, and most CS teams can't show it. That is why CS teams keep getting cut. When budgets tighten, a function that can't show its value to its own company is an easy line to remove. A function that can't show its value to customers either is in worse shape still.

## Usage is not value

Most CS teams measure adoption: logins, active seats, features touched. Adoption is easy to measure and easy to improve, so it becomes the target. But a customer doesn't renew because their seats are active. They renew because something in their business got better: invoices go out faster, the books close sooner, fewer hours go to rekeying data.

Adoption matters only when it produces those results. A team that reports adoption alone is reporting effort, not outcome. When the customer's CFO asks what they got for the money, "your users log in a lot" is not an answer.

## A menu, a baseline, and proof

The fix is not complicated, but it takes discipline.

**Offer a menu, not a blank page.** Write down the outcomes your product can actually move and you can actually measure. Keep the list short, about a dozen. Link each outcome to the features that drive it. This is an [Outcome Library](../frameworks/outcome-library.md). Its value is that it makes "what does success look like?" a choice, not a brainstorm.

**Let the customer pick 3 or 4.** Not everything at once: the ones that matter most where they are now. Fewer means too little proven value. More means adoption work spread too thin to move anything.

**Measure the baseline first.** Without a starting number there is nothing to prove later. A baseline taken six months in, after the product has already moved the number, gives away the credit you earned. Take it within 30 days of kickoff, from the same data source every later measurement will use.

**Measure automatically, and check the cause.** An outcome measured by a CSM's spreadsheet is an opinion. One measured from the system it lives in is evidence. Even then, a number can move for reasons that have nothing to do with you. So check whether the features behind the outcome are actually in use. If the number moved and the features didn't, you haven't proven anything yet. The repo calls that [Unexplained progress](../frameworks/outcome-library.md#checking-adoption-behind-each-outcome) and won't let you claim it.

**Bring it to every meeting.** QBRs, renewals, and expansion conversations all start from the same numbers: where you started, where you are, and where you're headed.

## Then sell it

Once you can reliably take a customer from baseline to a chosen outcome, you have something worth paying for. Call it [Adoption as a Service](../specs/adoption-as-a-service.md): a CS-led path to measured outcomes, sold at a price that covers the work.

The customer gets a lower-risk investment: someone accountable for turning the product into results. The company gets CS revenue, which is the plainest possible proof that the team is worth funding. Retention estimates always invite an argument about what would have happened anyway. Bookings don't.

Two guardrails keep this honest. Measurement stays free for every customer, because health scoring and renewals need it everywhere and value shouldn't sit behind a paywall. And the fee pays for the work, not for a guaranteed result, at least until you have enough history to know what you can promise.

## Alignment comes for free

A shared list of provable outcomes does something else: it gives every function the same definition of value.

- **Sales** sells outcomes from the list, with real ranges from past customers, and hands CS [what was sold](../playbooks/sales-to-cs-handoff.md), not a vague goal.
- **Product** sees which outcomes stall even when the features are used. That is the most actionable roadmap input there is, and the [Outcome Review](../playbooks/outcome-review.md) delivers it every quarter.
- **Marketing** gets proof points that are measured, attributable, and approved by the customer.
- **CS** gets a [value report](../tools/README.md#value-report) that shows what the team delivered across the book, in the customer's numbers.

## The question to ask

What stops your CS team from proving value? Missing data can be fixed. A narrow charter can be widened. Not knowing what customers want is solved by asking them to choose from a list. What's left is whether the team is willing to be measured on outcomes rather than activity. The teams that are will stop being at risk.
